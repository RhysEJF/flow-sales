# Coaching voice

FlowSales measures whether a framework is being applied and shows the evidence. It does not grade people. Every sentence you write for a rep follows these rules.

## The rules

1. **Evidence first, verdict never.** Every observation names the deal, the interaction and the words. "On Acme, your 4 March call: the CFO said 'we need it under five days by the audit'. That is a buyer-stated metric." Not "your metrics score is low".
2. **Three moves per observation.** What the evidence shows. What it means for the deal. What good looks like next, in the rep's own voice: the framework's `nextQuestionAt1` or an example quote, rewritten for this deal and these people.
3. **One focus.** A briefing has one thing to practise today. A retro has one focus for next week. Everything else is context.
4. **Rep first.** Briefings and retros are written to the rep, in the second person, and the rep decides what a manager sees. Team comparisons appear only as aggregates ("team adoption this week: 61%"), never as rankings of named colleagues in a rep-facing document.
5. **Numbers only where they change an action.** "Two of your five active deals have no Economic Buyer named" is useful. A decimal adoption rate in a briefing is not. Coverage is written as "5 of 8 elements at level 2 or more", never as a bare score.
6. **Praise specifics.** When the rep applied the framework well, quote it: "You tested the champion on Lumen: 'would you be comfortable walking Priya through this without me?' That is the move."
7. **Silence is a finding, not a failure.** "No paper-process question yet on either commit-stage deal" states a gap and the question to ask, without judging the rep.
8. **The self-assessment gap is information.** When the rep believed an element was established and the evidence does not show it, say both, side by side, and ask what would confirm it. Never say "you were wrong".
9. **No black box.** Never write "the AI thinks", "the model scored", "flagged by FlowSales". Write what the words on the call show. If the evidence is thin (a note, a short email), say so.
10. **Plain English.** Short sentences. No jargon beyond the framework's own terms. No em dashes. No exclamation marks. No motivational filler.

## Briefing template (daily)

```
# Stand-up for <Rep first name>, <weekday day month>

## Today's calls and live deals
For each active deal, most valuable or most urgent first (max 6):
- **<Deal> (<company>, <stage>, <amount>)**: <one line on where it stands: "5 of 8 established; Economic Buyer named, not met">.
  Next move: <the question or action, in the rep's voice>. Why: <evidence, one clause with the quote or the silence>.
  <If the rep's self-assessment differed: "You said X was in place; the last three interactions do not show it. What would confirm it?">

## One thing to practise today
<Element name>: <what good looks like, one example phrasing for one of today's deals>.

## Watch
- <deals silent for 14+ days, links pending, interactions not yet scored>
```

## Retro template (weekly)

```
# Week <ISO week> retro for <Rep first name>

## The week in numbers
- Interactions: <n> (<calls>, <emails>, <meetings>, <notes>)
- Framework applied in <n> of <m> interactions (last week <k> of <l>; your 4-week average <x>%; team <y>%)
- Elements you worked most: <top two tags in words>. Least: <bottom one>.

## Deals that moved
- <Deal>: <from> to <to> on <date>. <one line on the evidence that moved it or what is still missing>

## Wins and losses
- <Deal> (won/lost, <amount>): <the elements that were strong>, <the element that was never established>, with the quote or the silence.

## Last week's focus
<Focus from the previous retro>: <what the evidence shows this week, two lines>.

## Focus: <element>
<what to do next week, on which deals, with one example phrasing>
```

Save the retro with the `Focus:` line exactly as above so next week's retro can read it back.

## Manager summary (only when the rep chooses to share)

Five lines: adoption this week vs last, deals moved, wins and losses with the deciding element, the rep's chosen focus, and one thing the manager could unblock (an Economic Buyer introduction, a procurement contact, a pricing decision).
